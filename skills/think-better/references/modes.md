# Engagement Modes

After triage, select one mode. Tell the user which mode you're in before starting.

---

## Mode 1: Bias for Action
**When:** Reversible, low-stakes decision

**Opening:**
> "This is reversible and low-stakes. Let's move fast. One question:"

**Question bank (pick the most relevant, ask one at a time):**
- "What's stopping you from deciding right now?"
- "If you had to pick in the next 60 seconds, what would you choose?"
- "What would you need to see in 2 weeks to know it was the wrong call?"

**Exit condition:** User can state a decision and a success signal. Close with synthesis.

---

## Mode 2: Deep Dive
**When:** Irreversible or high-stakes decision

**Opening:**
> "This one deserves more care. Let's slow down. First question:"

**Question bank (ask one at a time, work through relevant ones):**

*Constraints:*
- "What are the hard constraints here — things that can't change regardless of what you decide?"
- "Who else is affected by this decision and how?"

*Second-order effects:*
- "If this works exactly as planned, what breaks next?"
- "What does the person most opposed to this decision say? Are they right about anything?"

*Assumptions:*
- "What are you assuming that you haven't verified?"
- "What would have to be true for the opposite choice to be correct?"

*Options:*
- "Have you considered doing nothing? What happens then?"
- "Is there a version of this that's smaller and reversible you could test first?"

*Probe variations (use when user needs to go deeper):*

**Devil's advocate:** "Take the opposite position of what you're leaning toward. Make the strongest possible case against it. I'll respond."

**Failure mode:** "If this decision turns out badly in six months, what are the most likely reasons why?"

**Blind spots:** "What important factors or perspectives are you not considering right now?"

**Exit condition:** User has surfaced key assumptions, considered opposing view, and can state their reasoning. Close with synthesis.

---

## Mode 3: Diverge First
**When:** Creative or generative problem (no clear right answer, need options)

**Opening:**
> "Let's generate before we narrow. Don't filter yet. First question:"

**Question bank:**
- "What are 3 completely different approaches, including one that feels too risky?"
- "What would you do if resources weren't a constraint?"
- "What would someone you admire do here?"
- "What's the most boring safe option and the most interesting risky one?"

After diverging (3+ options on the table):
> "Good. Now let's narrow. Which of these feels closest to right, and why?"

**Exit condition:** User has eliminated options with reasons and can defend their choice. Close with synthesis.

---

## Mode 4: Structured Extraction
**When:** Research or factual problem (user needs to know something before deciding)

**Opening:**
> "Before we can decide, we need to know a few things. Let's surface the gaps. First:"

**Question bank:**
- "What do you already know about this with confidence?"
- "What are you assuming but haven't verified?"
- "Where would the information you need actually come from?"
- "What's the minimum you need to know to make this call?"

**Exit condition:** User has a clear list of what they know, what they need to find out, and where to look. Close with synthesis — including explicit unknowns.

---

## Synthesis Template

Always end with:

> "Here's what we worked out:
> **Decision:** [what was decided]
> **Key reasons:** [2-3 reasons that drove the decision]
> **Ruled out:** [what was considered and rejected, and why]
> **Open questions:** [anything still unresolved that matters]"

If there are open questions, ask: "Do any of these need to be resolved before you move forward?"

**Stress test (optional, recommended for high-stakes decisions):**

After synthesis, ask: "Given this decision and reasoning, where is it still weak or unclear? What would you question if you were reviewing this critically?"
